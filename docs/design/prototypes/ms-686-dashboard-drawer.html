Why these two tickets are one

The desktop dashboard is the navigation. Eleven of its tiles are the only door to eleven pages, so every trip between two of them goes home first. The phone solved this years ago: the drawer is the navigation, and Home is a screen rather than a map. MS-691 brings that drawer to the desktop header — and the moment it lands, the tile grid is a second copy of the same list, which is what MS-686 has to answer.

The answer here is that the grid stays and earns its keep by getting smaller. A grid of eleven tiles is a board you read at a glance and point at; a drawer is a list you walk. They are worth having twice only if the board actually fits in one look — which the shipped tile does not allow, because three of them fill the fold. So the scale of everything on this page is the design decision, not a finishing pass over it.

A professional tool has small elements; a toy has big letters and big blocks. The desktop below is a step denser in every direction — type, padding, header height, controls, the Glance panel and the drawer itself — while the cream, the navy ink and the Cinzel / EB Garamond / Libre Franklin stack are untouched, because density is a matter of size and not of character. Nothing readable goes under 13 px, no desktop pointer target under 32 px, and the phone keeps the comfortable shipped values, 44 px targets included.

It wears the shipped design system — m-nav-card, m-medallion, m-card, m-notice, m-empty, m-row, m-badge, m-scripture, m-header — and the drawer is written as a proposed m-drawer in tokens, because the design system has no Drawer yet and the phone has two hand-styled copies of one. The density is var() overrides on the tokens those components already read, so it is a proposal the design system can adopt rather than a pile of magic numbers. Everybody named here is invented.

The dashboard, denser, with the drawer over it

Eleven tiles in one look Drawer matches the phone Phone scale unchanged

The hamburger goes in m-header__lead, left of the seal, and opens the phone’s drawer over the page you are on. The tiles become m-nav-card and m-medallion instead of eleven hand-written Tailwind card strings, and the floating 64 px logo moves into the header so the page can start with a sentence instead of a picture. The saved arrangement (dashboardCardOrder) survives untouched.

On the desktop the tile turns on its side: a 40 px Medallion at the left, the title and its reason beside it, padding two steps tighter. That is what buys all eleven tiles above the fold where three used to sit. The side column is a fixed 300 px rather than 4-of-12, because the Glance panel holds six label/value pairs and nothing longer, so past 300 px it is only a wider gutter. Narrow the window below 1024 px and every one of those overrides lifts: the tile goes back to the centred column with its 56 px plate, and the targets go back to 44 px. At the shipped tile size only three of these eleven cleared the fold; all eleven do now, in the order dashboardCardOrder already saves.

What I need decided

Four things the density raises that I should not settle on my own.

Does the density belong to the page or to the design system? Every override here is scoped to one stage. The same argument applies to Shepherd Dashboard, Printables, the Service editor — every desktop page in the app. Either build/design-tokens grows a desktop density scale that all of them inherit, or each component grows a --compact modifier and pages opt in. The first is one change and a lot of blast radius; the second is eight small changes and no surprises.
Is the compact tile a NavCard modifier or a second component? The row layout needs a wrapper NavCard does not have today (m-nav-card__body), so it is not a pure modifier. If the centred 56 px tile has no remaining caller once this lands, the honest move is to change NavCard itself rather than keep two shapes alive.
Eleven tiles, or the eleven you use? Density means the whole list now fits, so the grid shows everything and dashboardCardOrder keeps deciding the order. The alternative is that the grid becomes the short list — pinned or recent — and the drawer stays the complete one, which is a stronger reason for having both but a bigger change to what people already saved.
Does the seal move into the header? The restyle moves it there at 32 px, which costs the wordless 64 px mark its moment and gives the page a headline instead. index.html’s own comment says the seal is this page’s name; this disagrees with that comment and needs you to say so.

What I would do

Ship the density as component --compact modifiers rather than a global desktop scale, starting with the five this page actually needs — NavCard, Card, Header, Button and Row. It is the same visual result with a blast radius you can see, it lets the phone keep the shipped values by simply not opting in, and it gives the token set a chance to grow the two names that are missing (a control height and a row height) with real callers behind them rather than on spec.
