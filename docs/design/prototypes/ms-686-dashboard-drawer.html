Why these two tickets are one

The desktop dashboard is the navigation. Twelve of its tiles are the only door to twelve pages, so every trip between two of them goes home first. The phone solved this years ago: the drawer is the navigation, and Home is a screen rather than a map. MS-691 brings that drawer to the desktop header — and the moment it lands, the tile grid is a second copy of the same list, which is what MS-686 has to answer.

So the choice below is not a paint job. It is what the dashboard is for once the drawer carries the navigation: still the map (A), the week (B), or the same list read at a different size (C).

All three wear the shipped design system — m-nav-card, m-medallion, m-card, m-notice, m-empty, m-row, m-badge, m-scripture, m-header — and the drawer is written as a proposed m-drawer in tokens, because the design system has no Drawer yet and the phone has two hand-styled copies of one. Everybody named here is invented.

A · Overlay drawer, tile grid kept

Smallest change Matches the phone exactly Two copies of one list

The hamburger goes in m-header__lead, left of the seal, and opens the phone’s drawer over the page you are on. The dashboard keeps its 8/4 split and its tiles, but the tiles become m-nav-card and m-medallion instead of nine hand-written Tailwind card strings, and the floating 64 px logo moves into the header so the page can start with a sentence instead of a picture. The saved arrangement (dashboardCardOrder) survives untouched.

B · Docked rail, and the dashboard becomes the week

Navigation always visible Grouped, so twelve entries scan Deletes the tile grid

At 1024 px and up the drawer is docked as a 248 px rail and the hamburger collapses it to its glyphs; below 1024 px the same list becomes the overlay drawer from A, so the phone’s behaviour arrives at the bottom of the desktop range rather than at a device boundary. Because the list is permanently on screen, the tiles are pure duplication — so the dashboard stops being a map and answers the question people actually arrive with: what is happening, and what am I on?

You are reading this below 1024 px, so the rail is not on screen: both stages below have fallen back to A’s overlay drawer, which is exactly the point. Widen the window past 1024 px to see the rail itself.

What I need decided

Do the tiles live or die? A keeps them; B and C delete them. Deleting them also deletes the home of the saved arrangement — users.dashboardCardOrder and every data-card-key — so B and C need a decision about what happens to an arrangement somebody already dragged.
Flat like the phone, or grouped? The phone’s list holds ten and the desktop’s holds twelve for an Elder, thirteen for a super admin. A is flat for parity and is the weakest of the three to read. If grouped wins, destinations.js gains a group per entry and the phone inherits the grouping — that is a phone change inside a desktop ticket.
Are Shepherding and Admin groups of one? For an Elder they are: a heading over a single row, twice. Worth it, because elder-only work and church-wide settings are different kinds of thing and a super admin sees two rows under Admin — or should a group of one lose its heading and sit at the foot of the list?
Does the seal move into the header? All three move it there, which costs the 64 px wordless mark its moment and gives the page a headline instead. index.html’s comment says the seal is this page’s name; the restyle disagrees with that comment and needs you to say so.

Recommendation

C. It is the only one where the drawer and the dashboard cannot drift apart, it takes twelve entries without the eye sliding off, and it keeps the Sunday as the thing the page is about. B is the better desktop app and the worse first step: a docked rail changes every page’s width, not just this one. A is the safe half-measure — ship it and the duplication MS-686 exists to fix is still there, just prettier.
