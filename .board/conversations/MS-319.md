**MS-319 — "Create a family at the kiosk"** · Feature · To Plan · under epic MS-316, blocked by MS-318 (which is already in To Do).

The ticket itself is **sharp** — you wrote it well. It has a clear what-and-why and seven acceptance criteria. Left alone it'd go almost straight to a PRD. But three things in it don't survive contact with the code, and your add-on is a fourth.

**First, a correction.** Family is *not* a relationship type. It's its own Firestore collection with exactly four fields:

```
families/{id} = { husbandId, wifeId, childIds[], anniversary }
```

It only *looks* like a relationship type because `FamilyCore` synthesises read-only relation rows ("Husband", "Daughter") and two reserved group ids from it at read time. Nothing about a family is stored in `relationship_types`, `relationships`, or `relationship_groups` — and ADR-0014 explicitly rejected putting it there. So "buff the Family datatype" means adding fields to a real collection with five writers and a pure planner (`family-core.js`) that owns every household rule. Not a config tweak.

**The holes:**

1. **A family has two adult seats, not any number.** One male husband, one female wife. Your ticket says the dialog takes "any number of people, each adult-or-kid." Three adults at the desk — a couple and grandma — cannot be seated. Neither can two men. The planner refuses rather than guesses.

2. **There is nothing to search by surname.** MS-318's PRD promises "type a surname and see every matching family." A Person has one free-text `name` field — no first/last split, deliberately. A Family has no name at all; its display name is derived at read time ("Alice and Bob"). As specced today, MS-318 would ship a take-the-last-word hack. **This is exactly your instinct, and it's real.**

3. **A kiosk can't write.** MS-318 gave kiosk accounts *read* on `people` and `families` (ADR-0041). Writes are still `isEditor()`. Creating a family from the kiosk needs a new rule or a callable — undecided.

**My recommendation:** the Family-shape work is its own ticket, and it goes *first*. It's a schema change plus the planner, five call sites, the directory UI, and a backfill for every existing family. Folding it into MS-319 buries a directory-wide change inside a kiosk feature. And it changes what MS-318 builds — MS-318 is sitting in To Do right now and could get picked up any morning with the surname hack baked in.

## Round 1

❓ **Q1 — What is a family called?**

Today a Family has no name. The label you see — "Alice and Bob" — is assembled on the fly from whoever is in it. That's fine for the directory and useless for a greeter who wants to type "Nguyen" and find a household.

(a) **A Family Name.** One free-text line per household. At the kiosk it's prefilled with the last word of the first adult's name, so the greeter usually just tabs past it, and it's editable in the directory afterwards. Existing families get backfilled the same way.
(b) **No new field.** Search matches the last word of each member's name. Nothing to store, nothing to backfill.
(c) **A surname on every Person.** Split the name field properly. Biggest change, and it undoes a decision the model made on purpose.

➡️ **(a).** (b) breaks the moment a household's names don't share a word — a blended family, a wife who kept her name, or anywhere the family name comes first. Those aren't edge cases in a church foyer. (a) costs the greeter about two seconds and gives an editor something to fix when it's wrong.

---

❓ **Q2 — Three adults walk in together.**

A Family has exactly two adult seats: one husband who must be male, one wife who must be female. Your ticket promises the dialog takes "any number of people." A couple with their kids fits. A couple, their kids, and grandma does not.

(a) **Keep two seats.** Grandma gets created as a Person, told plainly she wasn't added to the household, left for an editor.
(b) **Give a Family other adults.** A new list alongside husband and wife, for someone who lives in the household without being married into it. Nothing that reads the existing fields breaks.
(c) **Replace the seats with a flat list of adults.** Honest, and a multi-week rewrite touching the relations panel, the serving rules and the Relations Viewer.

➡️ **(b).** Attendance is precisely the case where "who walked in together" matters more than "who is married to whom", and a kiosk will meet grandmothers, adult sons still at home and lodgers constantly. Under (a) grandma silently falls out of the household, which breaks your own demo — mark them present, then find them in the directory. (c) is a different project.

❓ **Q3 — They hit Create when the family already exists.**

Your ticket says a duplicate must not corrupt the original, and an editor cleans it up later. Agreed. The question is whether we say anything first.

(b) **Never warn.** Fastest.
(c) **Block on an exact name match.** Never — it makes two real Smith families impossible.

➡️ **(a).** A warning after an empty search is insulting; after a full one it's the whole point. Costs nothing when it isn't needed.

You said "Houses", but when you named one you wrote *"The Harris Household."*

(a) **Household.** The word you reached for naturally, and the word a greeter says out loud. Family's entry gets rewritten to say "family" where it currently says "household", and the Avoid line flips over.

---

❓ **Q2 — Does the kiosk also build a family tree, or only a household?**

The kiosk sees two adults and two kids standing together. It does not know the adults are married, or that they're the kids' parents. Two brothers share a house. A grandmother brings her grandchildren.

(a) **Household only.** Kinship stays blank until someone who actually knows fills it in from the directory.
(b) **Household plus a guessed family** — seat the adults as spouses, the kids as children.

➡️ **(a).** Everywhere else, this model refuses to guess a marriage rather than record a wrong one — the quick-assign card already refuses a household join it can't be sure of. ❓ **Q3 — Where does "kid" live?**

(a) **On the membership** — "this person is a child *in this household*." Doesn't claim to know their age, and an editor flips it when they grow up.
(b) **On the Person** — a flag that quietly rots, because nobody comes back to clear it on a birthday.
(c) **Ask for a birth year instead** — accurate forever, and a fifth question at the desk.

➡️ **(a).** It's exactly what the greeter is asserting and all the name tag needs. (c) fights the ticket's whole point.

❓ **Q4 — Day one, the kiosk searches and finds almost nothing.**

Every existing person is in a Family or in nothing. No households exist yet.

(a) **Build a household for every existing family, and let the search also find loose people on their own.** A single adult with no family still comes up.
(b) **Build a household for every existing person too** — thousands of one-person households, most of them noise forever.
(c) **No backfill.** The kiosk is empty until greeters have typed the whole church in by hand.

➡️ **(a).** (c) makes the feature useless for a year. (b) fills the directory with junk to avoid one fallback in the search.

Your acceptance criterion says a duplicate is fine because an editor cleans it up later. Nothing today would let them.

(a) **The Membership Directory shows a person's household and lets an editor rename it, move people between households, and merge two.** Real scope — a new section on the People page.

A child of separated parents genuinely is. But at the kiosk they'd then show up under both.

(a) **Allow it in the data, don't let the kiosk create it.** The kiosk only ever puts brand-new people into a brand-new household, so it can't cause this; an editor can when it's true.
(b) **One household each, enforced.** Simpler, and wrong for a real family the church will meet.

➡️ **(a).** Costs nothing now and doesn't paint us into a corner.
